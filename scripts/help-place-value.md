# Help Lesson Script: Place Value

**Topic:** Place Value (Tens and Ones)
**Duration:** ~80 seconds
**Character:** Teacher (friendly, encouraging)

---

## SCRIPT

### INTRO (5 sec)

**[VISUAL: Teacher character appears]**

**TEACHER:** "Let's discover something clever about numbers!"

---

### PART 1: The Problem (15 sec)

**[VISUAL: 23 individual dots scattered on screen - messy and hard to count]**

**TEACHER:** "How many dots are here? Try counting them..."

**[VISUAL: Dots wiggle slightly - it's confusing]**

**TEACHER:** "It's hard, right? There's too many! It's messy."

---

### PART 2: The Solution - Groups of 10 (25 sec)

**[VISUAL: The dots start organising into groups of 10 - forming two neat groups of 10, with 3 left over]**

**TEACHER:** "But watch what happens when we put them in groups of TEN..."

**[VISUAL: Two groups of 10 dots (maybe in circles or boxes), plus 3 loose ones]**

**TEACHER:** "Now I can see: 2 groups of ten... and 3 extras."

**[VISUAL: The number 23 appears]**

**TEACHER:** "That's 23! The 2 means 2 tens. The 3 means 3 ones."

**[VISUAL: Highlight the 2, then highlight the 3]**

**TEACHER:** "TWENTY... three. Two tens, three ones."

---

### PART 3: Why Position Matters (20 sec)

**[VISUAL: Show 32 next to 23]**

**TEACHER:** "Here's the tricky part. Look at these two numbers."

**[VISUAL: 32 on left, 23 on right - same digits, different order]**

**TEACHER:** "They use the same digits - 2 and 3. But they're NOT the same!"

**[VISUAL: Under 32, show 3 tens and 2 ones. Under 23, show 2 tens and 3 ones]**

**TEACHER:** "32 is THREE tens and two ones. 23 is TWO tens and three ones."

**[VISUAL: Show the actual amounts - 32 is clearly bigger]**

**TEACHER:** "WHERE the number sits tells you what it's worth!"

---

### SUMMARY (15 sec)

**[VISUAL: A simple place value chart - Tens | Ones - with 45 broken into 4 and 5]**

**TEACHER:** "So remember - we group by tens to make counting easier."

**[VISUAL: Point to tens column]**

**TEACHER:** "This side tells us how many tens..."

**[VISUAL: Point to ones column]**

**TEACHER:** "This side tells us the leftovers - the ones."

**[VISUAL: Teacher thumbs up]**

**TEACHER:** "That's place value!"

**[VISUAL: Fade out]**

---

## VISUAL ASSETS NEEDED

1. Teacher character (existing)
2. Dots/counters (individual, then grouping animation)
3. Groups of 10 visual (circles, ten-frames, or boxes)
4. Place value chart (Tens | Ones columns)
5. Number comparison visual (32 vs 23)
6. Base-10 blocks could work well here too

## VOICE NOTES

- Make the "messy dots" feel genuinely hard to count
- The "aha moment" is when they group into tens
- Emphasise the word "tens" and "ones"
- The 32 vs 23 comparison is the key insight

## KEY INSIGHT

Kids often just see 23 as "two, three" - they don't understand that the 2 is worth TWENTY. This lesson shows WHY we group by 10s (it makes counting easier) and that position changes value.

## CONNECTS TO

- Base-10 blocks questions
- "How many tens and ones?" questions
- Expanded form (45 = 40 + 5)
- Adding two-digit numbers (you add tens to tens, ones to ones)
